Restraining order forms, state by state

Every state calls it something different, but the packet is the same five or six pieces. The record fills the same parts of each one, and refuses the same parts.

What every state asks for

Names change, the job doesn't.

The pieceWhat it isWhat the record fills
The petitionCalled a request, petition, complaint or application. Where you describe what happened.The dated incidents, quoted, most recent first, with the file and timestamp behind each line.
The sworn declarationAn affidavit or attached statement when the box runs out of room. Signed under oath.The full chronology and exhibits, page numbered with the file fingerprint in the footer.
The confidential information sheetThe restrained person's details, for police. The other side never sees it.Blank. Dates of birth, vehicles and plates are almost never in a message record, and a guess on a police form is worse than an empty box.
The temporary orderWhat the judge signs the same day.Nothing. What to ask the judge for is a legal decision.
Notice of hearingCourt fills most of it.Party names only.
Proof of serviceSigned by whoever serves the papers.Nothing.
Firearms surrenderSeparate in many states, and often has its own deadline.Flagged, never filled. Raised only where the record actually mentions a weapon.

What travels between states

The record itself The dated chronology Exhibits with their fingerprints The gap, stated on its face

The evidence does not change when you cross a state line, only the boxes it goes in. An order granted in one state is enforceable in the others, and registering it is its own short form.
