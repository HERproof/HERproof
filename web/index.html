How the encryption works

The threat model is her own home: a partner who checks her phone, an account in his name, a lawyer on the other side who will subpoena anything that exists. So the design makes most of that impossible rather than promising to behave.

At rest
Your PIN makes the key The vault key is derived from your PIN on your own machine and never sent anywhere. We do not hold it, cannot reset it and cannot open your vault. If you lock it, it is unreadable even to us.
In transit
Sealed to your attorney Before anything leaves, the packet is encrypted again to your attorney's public key. What crosses the network is ciphertext, and only their private key opens it. No server in the middle can read it.
At our end
There is no copy We keep no copy of your files, your record or your forms. There is nothing on our side to hack, to hand over, or for anyone to subpoena from us, because it was never there.

What that means in practice. Delete removes the file from this machine rather than hiding it from you. Every item keeps the fingerprint taken when it arrived, so an attorney can show it has not been edited since. Reading happens against the files on your machine, and the extraction worker keeps nothing after a run. Today this runs locally end to end; the key handling is built into the flow you can see, and an independent security review is the next step before anyone relies on it in a real case.
